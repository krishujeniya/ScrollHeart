# Phase 5B: Post-Build Guidance

> This is the most important phase. The code is done, but the gesture isn't complete until she sees it.
> Coach the user on HOW to present it, WHEN to send it, and how to handle ANY outcome.
> Always end with emotional validation.

---

## How to Present the Site to Her

### The Message to Send

Tailor the message based on the `relationship_stage` from the interview:

**Best Friends:**
> "Hey, I made something small for you. It's just a little story about how much I appreciate you. No need to respond right away — just open it when you have a quiet moment. 💫"

**Crush (figuring it out):**
> "I've been wanting to tell you something, and I'm better with code than words. I made this for you. Take your time with it. 😊"

**Early Dating:**
> "I wanted to do something a little different for you. Open it when you're free and have a few minutes. I hope it makes you smile. ❤️"

**Committed:**
> "You know I'm not great with words, so I built them instead. This is for you. I love you. 💫"

**Presentation tips to share with the user:**
1. Send the message as a **direct text or DM** — not in a group chat
2. Add the link on a **separate line** so it stands out
3. Keep the message **short** — let the site do the talking
4. Include **"no pressure"** or **"take your time"** language
5. Don't say "I spent 3 hours on this" — it creates obligation

---

## When to Send It

> "Timing matters almost as much as the message itself."

**Best times to send:**
- Evening, when she's likely relaxed and alone
- Weekend afternoon, when she has time to scroll slowly
- After a good conversation — the emotional momentum helps

**Worst times to send:**
- During work or school hours
- Right after an argument or tension
- At midnight (feels urgent/anxious)
- Right before she has plans (she can't absorb it properly)
- On a day where she's clearly stressed

**Holiday timing:**
- Don't make it a holiday gift unless it was specifically made for that holiday
- If it's close to Valentine's Day, her birthday, or an anniversary, frame it accordingly
- But ScrollHeart sites are designed to feel timeless — they work any day

## Sharing Protocol

Tell the user:

> "Here are some ground rules for sharing:"

1. **Private first** — send the link to her directly, 1-on-1
2. **No social media** — never post on Instagram, WhatsApp status, Twitter, or any public feed
3. **No group chats** — this is between you two
4. **She decides** — if she wants to share it with her friends, that's her choice. Not yours.
5. **No screenshot of her reaction** — that's private too

## QR Code Alternative

If the user wants a physical touch:

> "Here's something really special: instead of texting the link, you could print a QR code and give it to her on a handwritten note. It bridges the digital and physical — and feels incredibly intentional."

**How to generate a QR code:**
1. Go to `qr-code-generator.com` or similar free tool
2. Paste the site URL
3. Download the QR code image
4. Print it on a small card or write a note around it

**Example note:**
> "I made you something. Scan this when you have a quiet moment. 💫"

## Handling Every Outcome

### If She Loves It

> "That's amazing! Here's what to do next:"

1. **Thank her** for taking the time to look at it
2. **Keep it light** — don't immediately ask "So what are we?"
3. **Suggest something IRL** — "Want to grab coffee this weekend?" builds on the momentum
4. **Keep the site live** — she might want to revisit it
5. **Don't screenshot her reaction** — let the moment be private

### If She Doesn't Respond Immediately

> "This is the hardest part. But here's what to remember:"

1. **Wait at least 24 hours** — she may need time to process
2. **Don't double-text** — one follow-up max, and only after 24+ hours
3. **Don't stalk the GitHub analytics** — seriously, don't
4. **Possible reasons for silence:**
   - She's busy (most common)
   - She's shy and doesn't know how to respond
   - She needs time to process her own feelings
   - She hasn't opened it yet
5. **What NOT to say in a follow-up:**
   - ❌ "Did you see it?"
   - ❌ "Why haven't you responded?"
   - ❌ "I spent a lot of time on that..."
6. **What's okay to say after 24+ hours:**
   - ✅ "No pressure at all — just wanted to make sure the link worked. 😊"
7. **Be proud regardless** — you did something brave

### If She's Uncomfortable

> "This is rare, but it's important to handle with grace."

1. **Apologize sincerely** — "I'm sorry if that felt like too much. I just wanted you to know I care about you."
2. **Don't explain or defend** — "I didn't mean it that way" can feel dismissive
3. **Give space** — don't push for a conversation about it
4. **Delete the site** — go to GitHub → Settings → Delete this repository
5. **Learn from it** — reflect on whether the intensity matched the relationship stage
6. **It's okay** — not every gesture lands. Your intention was kind.

### If She Says "Let's Just Be Friends"

> "That takes a lot of courage for her too. Here's how to handle it:"

1. **Respect it** — "I appreciate you being honest. That means a lot."
2. **Don't argue or try to convince** — that crosses a line
3. **Give yourself time** — it's okay to feel disappointed
4. **The friendship is worth protecting** — if it was good before, it can be good again
5. **Delete or keep the site** — your choice. If keeping it feels painful, delete it

## When to Delete the Repository

Tell the user:

> "A few thoughts on when to clean up:"

| Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| She loved it and you're together | Keep it — it's a memory now |
| She loved it but you're still friends | Keep it — it's a kind gesture either way |
| She didn't respond after a week | Keep it for now — she may still find it |
| She was uncomfortable | Delete immediately as a sign of respect |
| 6 months have passed | Consider deleting as a privacy practice |
| The relationship has changed | Delete if the content no longer feels appropriate |

**How to delete:**
1. Go to the repository on GitHub
2. Click **Settings**
3. Scroll to the bottom → **Danger Zone** → **Delete this repository**
4. Confirm the repository name
5. It's gone permanently

## Edge Cases

### She asks "Did someone help you make this?"
Suggest the user say:
> "Yeah, I used an AI tool to help with the design. But every word, every memory, every choice — that was all me."

### She wants to show it to her friends
> "That's her call, not yours. If she wants to share, let her. Just don't be the one posting it publicly."

### She wants you to make one for her friend
> "That's sweet! You can use ScrollHeart again to make a new one. Every output is unique."

### The user wants to make changes after deployment
> "I can help you update it. Just tell me what you want to change, and I'll modify the code. Then re-upload to GitHub."

### The user regrets sending it
> "It's natural to feel vulnerable after putting yourself out there. But the gesture was kind, and you can't unsend it. If you want, you can delete the repository and the link will stop working."
